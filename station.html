From the Shack

A brief journal of the shack, my operating philosophy, and the occasional rabbit hole.

October 2026

It’s been a minute since my last update. I took a bit of a step back from radio over the past year to focus on family, coach my son’s soccer team, and catch up on a few projects, both personal and radio-related.

Back in May, I picked up a “for parts” PX3 and discovered that all it needed was a new capacitor. I never realized how useful a panadapter could be until I started using the PX3. I’m still learning all the ways I can use it, but I really appreciate being able to turn on the rig and quickly assess band activity before tuning around.

For those wondering, yes, the entire setup is still running off a 1212A Bioenno battery, which I only need to charge about once a month.

Over the past year, I also found that while the overhead operating position of the KX3/PX3 was good, it wasn’t quite ideal for my deep desk. I modeled a few stands in Fusion360 based on the Nifty Accessories Desk Stands for the KX3 and PX3 to clean up the wires and improve the operating angle.

For those interested, you can download the .STL files here and print your own.

December 2025

The shack setup has been working great so far, and I have absolutely zero complaints with the KX3. Initially, I thought I would want the optional roofing filter, but after putting it through a few contests, I really don’t see the need for one right now.

As for keys, the AME Porta Paddle performed great, but it definitely left me curious about what I might be missing with a higher-end key. Staying with the made-in-USA theme, I had been keeping my feelers out for an N3ZN paddle when one caught my attention in a QRZ ad.

The ZN-Mini is the newest addition to the shack, and has officially pushed the Porta Paddle into the POTA bag. This particular ZN-Mini came with a solid base, not as heavy as the full-size N3ZN keys, but more than enough for the way I operate.

June 2025

Since setting up my shack in the garage back in March, a friend gave me a good length of RG-58, so I decided to extend the feedline and move the setup more permanently into my home office.

Lately, I’ve also been thinking more about what I want from the shack and have settled on something very simple, yet capable. The one minor challenge is that I’ve decided to primarily use products made here in the USA. That decision was largely driven by wanting the confidence that parts and service will remain available over the long term.

The biggest change to the shack was picking up a second-hand KX3, which has considerably simplified the overall footprint of the setup.

February 2025

I moved to Idaho back in November and am just getting around to setting up my first shack. Until now, I’ve been operating portable, so I’m excited to finally have a dedicated space to tinker with.

For now, I’m using equipment I already had on hand: a Yaesu FT-817ND, LDG Z817 tuner, and AME Porta Paddle. That’s also why the entire setup runs off a 1212A Bioenno battery. There’s no need to take up space with a power supply when the battery runs everything just fine for weeks at a time based on my usage.

The setup in my garage is something reminiscent of my college dorm room circa 2006. Coming from SOTA/POTA, I’m most familiar with EFHW antennas, so I went with what I know and ran 66 feet of wire from my roofline to a large white maple in the yard.
