CW

Morse code has always appealed to me because of its simplicity. A radio, an antenna, a key, and a few watts are enough to have a conversation with another operator almost anywhere in the world.

I enjoy CW for what it is rather than as a means of collecting awards or achievements. A simple QSO with another operator can be a satisfying reason to sit down at the radio.

Operating CW

My preferred contacts are generally straightforward: callsigns, signal reports, names, locations, equipment, antennas, and a few pleasantries.

There's something enjoyable about slowing down and having a simple conversation without needing to chase a particular number or accomplishment.

Keys and Paddles

This page will eventually include information about the keys and paddles I use, along with some thoughts about different styles of sending.

Learning and Improving

CW is one of those skills that can always be improved. The goal isn't necessarily to become exceptionally fast. Being comfortable enough to copy and send naturally is more important to me.

The Simple QSO

A short conversation between two operators is enough. No award, contest, special event, or rare DX is required to make a contact worthwhile.
