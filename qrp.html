QRP

There is something deeply gratifying about making contact with someone thousands of miles away using only a few watts and a relatively simple station. Your small signal travels across the world, reaches someone else's receiver, and their signal makes its way back to you. It's really that simple.

QRP Philosophy

QRP represents a particular mindset and approach to amateur radio, but I believe also flows over into my daily life. When I turn on my radio, life slows down. I’m not in a rush. When I turn on my radio, I’m looking for less distraction. I’m looking for simple, unadulterated child-like curiosity and excitement. I’m looking to have fun.

I was initially drawn to radio by the thought of using technology that has been around for decades to connect with people around the world. Unlike a cell phone, where communication is almost effortless, radio makes you work for it.

There is something appealing about that. Radio brings back some of the excitement of making a connection without the convenience of modern technology. It connects you with people who share an interest in the equipment, the technology, and the experience of connecting with someone you may or may not know somewhere you may or may not have ever been before.

QRP adds another layer to that experience. It brings back a sense of uncertainty and discovery that makes each contact feel just that much more rewarding. After briefly experimenting with higher power levels, I noticed that I enjoyed my QSOs less. Sure, in general using more power made making contacts easier, but at the same time, it also took away some of the excitement. I wanted something different: a way to make my experience more engaging. I wanted success to be a little less certain and each contact to require a little more effort. QRP introduces that challenge.
