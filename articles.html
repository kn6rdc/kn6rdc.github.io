QRP as I Have Experienced It

My journey into QRP deserves a bit of background. I found my way into the world of amateur radio through a series of events, failures, and choices.

I earned my Technician license in 2021 and immediately gravitated toward SOTA (Summits on the Air). Living in Southern California, I found that activating many summits on 2-meter VHF was both practical and achievable.

I attempted my first seven peaks using only an HT. Six of those attempts resulted in successful activations. The one that stopped me was in an awkward geographic location that didn't offer the broad line of sight needed for a successful 2-meter activation.

That experience pushed me to explore other options. After talking with Kevin KN6FNY, my neighbor at the time, I was introduced to CW and QRP as alternatives to the more conventional approach of SSB and 100 watts.

I was a little hesitant, but I was also excited to try something new. I dove in.

CW gave me the edge I needed to stick with QRP, and I've never looked back. Over the years, QRP operation has greatly simplified the hobby for me. It gives me constraints that I honestly thrive on, providing just enough resistance to keep me interested in chasing that next QSO time and time again.

To me, QRP is about more than operating at low power. It makes me think about my footprint, how I can do more with less, and how I can simplify my approach. It also forces me to slow down, something I think we could all use amid today's constant hustle.

QRP is simply more fun.
